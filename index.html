Common questions

Why does the app split retinol from carotenoid?

Because they behave differently. Preformed retinol — from liver, fish liver oils, fortified foods, eggs and dairy — is absorbed efficiently and stored in the liver. Provitamin A carotenoids from plants are converted at roughly 12:1 for beta-carotene, and that conversion is regulated.

The tolerable upper intake level — 3,000 mcg a day for adults — applies to preformed retinol only. Beta-carotene from food does not cause vitamin A toxicity. A single combined number cannot tell you which half you are near the limit on, so the app shows preformed retinol on its own counter.

A food I eat isn't in the database

Use Food → Custom food to enter values per serving. If you leave the preformed retinol field blank, the whole total is counted as preformed — the cautious assumption for the upper limit. For packaged and fortified foods, the nutrition label is more accurate than any database.

Searches that come back empty are recorded under More → Your data and can be sent in one tap.

Where do the numbers come from?

Food values come from USDA FoodData Central and are checked against it. They are averages and will not match every package, animal or cultivar: fortification varies by brand and country, and dairy and egg yolks vary with feed.

Reference intakes and the upper limit come from the Institute of Medicine Dietary Reference Intakes and the EFSA scientific opinion on preformed vitamin A. Every source is linked in the app under More → Field notes.

Which protocol should I choose?

Reference is the suggested start: the recommended intake (900 mcg RAE a day for men, 700 for women), to see where your intake actually sits. Upper limit, Pregnancy and Bone health add the relevant ceilings on preformed retinol.

Reduced intake and Stepped reduction let you go below the reference intake. No clinical guideline specifies a target below the recommended amount, so if you do this, do it with a clinician and with bloodwork. If you are working with a clinician, use Set my own for their number.

Why does the app track calcium?

The foods richest in preformed retinol — dairy and eggs — are also where most diets get their calcium, so cutting one tends to cut the other by accident.

The meal plans show their calcium totals up front. Sardines with bones, chia, almonds, sesame, white beans, calcium-set tofu, fortified plant milks and hard mineral water carry calcium with little or no vitamin A.

My symptom journal shows nothing useful yet

It needs roughly two weeks of scores before the trend line means anything. The value of the record is retrospective — when something shifts weeks later, the change markers let you look back and see what you did.

I'm switching phones / I lost my data

Your data lives only on your phone. Before you switch, use More → Your data → Export JSON and keep the file — that is your backup. Deleting the app deletes the data with it.

If data is already gone with no export, it cannot be recovered. We are sorry; that is the direct cost of the app holding nothing about you on any server of ours.

This app is a food-logging tool, not medical advice.

Vitamin A is an essential nutrient, and deficiency causes serious harm. There are real clinical reasons to limit preformed vitamin A — retinoid therapy, liver disease and pregnancy among them — but no guideline specifies an intake below the recommended amount. This app does not diagnose, treat, cure or prevent any disease.

Signs worth taking to a doctor: changes in night vision or dry eyes, unusual bruising or bleeding gums, fatigue that deepens rather than lifts, bone pain, or unintended weight loss.

If you are pregnant, nursing, feeding a child, or managing a diagnosed condition, speak to a clinician before restricting any nutrient.
